Refunds on orders that were never shipped are taken off the revenue, because that money was never really earned; they stay visible in their own row so you can keep monitoring them as lost revenue. The loss percentage then covers shipped orders only. Every store uses the same definitions and cut-off date, so the numbers compare directly. A new store appears here as soon as its files are added.

Where the money went

Everything that left the business through refunds, chargebacks and disputes. Refunds are split into orders that were never (fully) shipped, which are supplier or stock issues, and orders that were shipped.

    How this report is built

    • Lost is refunds plus chargebacks plus chargeback and dispute fees, minus money won back. Profit, cost of goods and advertising are not part of this report.
    • Never (fully) shipped means the order has no fulfilment, or at least one line was never fulfilled. A refund on such an order counts as a supplier or stock issue. A refund whose note names stock, color, size or a late shipment counts as a supplier issue as well.
    • Refunds are the amounts the payment providers actually paid back: Shopify Payments refunds, PayPal refunds, and manual refunds recorded in Shopify. PayPal refunds made outside Shopify, for example after a dispute, are included.
    • Chargebacks are the amounts Shopify Payments debited, plus the fee per chargeback, minus chargebacks won. PayPal disputes are the reversals and dispute fees in the PayPal account. Refunds made to settle a PayPal dispute are counted once, as refunds.
    • Problem rate is the share of orders with a refund, a chargeback or a PayPal case. An order counts once.
    • Cut-off date: every source is cut at the same date. Status of PayPal cases is as of the day the case report was downloaded.
    • Adding a store: send the same files for the new store and a new tab appears. The PayPal export can hold several stores. They are split on the store ID that PayPal records on every payment.
